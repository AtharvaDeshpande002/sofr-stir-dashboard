SOFR / STIR TRADING DASHBOARD — PRESENTATION SCRIPT
====================================================

For: a Claude agent that will turn this into presentation points / slides.
Purpose: show what I built, why it matters for trading, and what it says about how I work.
Tone: a portfolio / resume piece. Explain importance and usefulness, not implementation detail.
Please do not include positions, P&L figures, firm names, network paths or any client data.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHO I AM AND WHAT I TRADE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

I am a proprietary trader in US short-term interest rate futures (STIR):
  - SR3 — 3-month SOFR futures (the market's view of the Fed's rate path, quarter by quarter)
  - ZQ  — 30-day Fed Funds futures (pricing individual FOMC meetings)
  - VX  — VIX futures (volatility term structure)

My style is relative value on the mid and long end of the curve: spreads, butterflies,
double flies and multi-leg packages, rather than outright direction. The edge in this kind
of trading comes from knowing, at any moment, which part of the curve is cheap or rich
against its neighbours, its own history and the economic calendar — and from acting with
discipline when that is the case.

No off-the-shelf platform gave me that view in one place, so I designed and built my own.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE PROBLEM
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Before the dashboard, a typical morning meant:
  - several Excel workbooks (live prices, settles, positions, P&L) open at once;
  - mentally converting outright prices into spreads, flies and structures;
  - no quick way to answer "is this fly expensive versus its history?" or "how did this part
    of the curve behave on the last five hot CPI prints?";
  - risk spread across many structures, with no single picture of where the book was
    actually exposed on the curve;
  - historical data refreshed by hand.

Result: slow decisions, decisions driven by feel, and mistakes that come from fatigue —
especially trading US hours from India.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE SOLUTION IN ONE SENTENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

A single browser-based trading cockpit that combines live market data, years of settlement
history, my book, the economic calendar and quantitative models — so every trade starts from
evidence, has a defined risk, and can be explained to a mentor in a few lines.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW IT IS BUILT (KEEP THIS HIGH LEVEL)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Live layer: a small Python service reads live prices, settlements, meeting pricing, VIX
    and my positions straight from the trading workbook every few seconds and publishes them
    to the dashboard. The dashboard updates without anyone touching Excel.
  - History layer: a self-updating market database (years of SR3, VIX and economic-release
    history) is exported automatically when a new day is published. No manual refresh.
  - Front end: one fast HTML/JavaScript application with charts, served on the office
    network so it opens in any browser. Private data (positions, P&L) stays on my machine.
  - Analytics: statistics, ranges, z-scores, mean-reversion half-lives, principal component
    analysis (PCA), walk-forward backtests and event studies, all computed in the browser.
  - AI agents: specialised Claude agents that write a daily curve report, an open-interest
    report and a mentor-ready "trade check" for any structure I'm considering.
  - Design: a calm, compact "graphite" theme built for long sessions — information-dense
    without scrolling, colour used only where it carries meaning.

Skills shown: market structure knowledge, quantitative modelling, Python, JavaScript,
data engineering, UI/UX design, and using AI agents as part of a real trading workflow.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
5. THE TABS — WHAT EACH DOES AND WHY IT MATTERS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

The dashboard is grouped into three sections: LIVE, ANALYSIS and TRADING.

LIVE ------------------------------------------------------------------

5.1 CURVE
  What: the live SOFR curve of 3-month spreads against yesterday's settlement, with my
  positions drawn on it and a live P&L chart split by structure.
  Why it matters: one glance tells me what the market did today, where I am exposed and
  which structure is making or losing money. Positions are shown decomposed into the 3M
  spreads they really represent, so hidden concentrations become visible.

5.2 OPPORTUNITIES
  What: an always-on scanner with two independent methods.
    - Range signals: flags structures stretched to the edge of their own recent range,
      with an honest track record of how that rule has performed on each structure.
    - PCA rich/cheap: models the whole curve's common movements and ranks structures by
      what the model cannot explain — genuine relative-value dislocations.
  Built-in safeguards: robustness across model settings, an out-of-sample (walk-forward)
  backtest, a regime check against the 2022–23 Fed hiking cycle, cost-adjusted "net edge",
  stop guidance from history, and grouping so one dislocation isn't counted ten times.
  Why it matters: it turns "I think this looks cheap" into a ranked, tested, cost-aware list
  — and, just as importantly, tells me when there is no trade.

5.3 ZQ MEETINGS
  What: live pricing of each upcoming FOMC meeting and the meeting-to-meeting structures
  (next meeting vs the one after, ratios).
  Why it matters: shows exactly which meeting the market is repricing, which explains a lot
  of the curve's daily moves.

5.4 VIX
  What: the VIX futures curve with spreads, flies, double flies and ratio structures, plus
  seasonality — how the same structure behaved in previous years as expiry approached —
  with percentile rankings and interactive filtering of outlier years.
  Why it matters: volatility structures are seasonal and expiry-driven; this puts today's
  price in that context immediately.

ANALYSIS --------------------------------------------------------------

5.5 SCENARIO
  What: an interactive sandbox. Drag FOMC meeting outcomes, roll, shift or twist the curve,
  and see how every structure — and my book — would reprice. A "Best Trades" view then
  ranks the structures that benefit most from the scenario I built.
  Why it matters: lets me plan trades around a view ("what if CPI is hot and the Fed hikes
  in December?") before the event, rather than react after it.

5.6 SR3 HISTORY
  What: the full curve on any two dates side by side, from the settlement database.
  Why it matters: quick answers to "what did the curve look like before the last hiking
  cycle?" or "how much has this part of the curve moved since the last FOMC?".

5.7 T&S HISTORY
  What: time-and-sales analysis — where buying and selling volume has been concentrated —
  plus a live flow strip.
  Why it matters: shows who is active and where, so I can tell a real move from thin
  liquidity.

5.8 ECON
  What: the economic calendar with actual vs consensus history for every major release, and
  an Event Playbook: on past hot and soft prints (CPI, payrolls, PCE…), how every part of the
  curve moved, how reliably, and what that would mean for my current book.
  Why it matters: this market moves on data. Knowing in advance which structures react, and
  by how much, is the difference between planning a trade and gambling on a number.

TRADING ---------------------------------------------------------------

5.9 RV BUILDER
  What: build any multi-leg package from spreads and flies, with its exact contract order,
  its net exposure in 3-month spreads, its full history on the actual contracts (correctly
  handling quarterly rolls), range statistics, mean-reversion speed and sensitivity to the
  rate level. Extra views find delta-neutral combinations and compare the same package
  across every point of the curve.
  Why it matters: this is where an idea becomes a precise, sized, hedged trade.

5.10 TRADE
  What: a trade-planning board — scaling-in ladders, exits and stop levels for a position.
  Why it matters: risk and exits are decided before entry, not during the trade.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HOW I USE IT IN A TRADING DAY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Check the data is live.
  2. Context: what moved, what the Fed meetings price, what data is due (Curve, ZQ, Econ).
  3. Candidates: Opportunities (PCA and range), with robustness and track record.
  4. Stress-test: does it survive other model settings, the hiking-cycle comparison,
     fixed-contract history, and is there a real reason (meeting, event, flow) behind it?
  5. Price it in the RV Builder against trading costs; check contract limits.
  6. Write the plan — entry, target, stop, time stop — and get a mentor-ready summary from the
     trade-check agent.
  7. Execute, then monitor on Curve and the residual history until target, stop or time.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Built by the trader who uses it: every feature answers a real question I had at the desk.
  - Honest analytics: in-sample vs out-of-sample is explicit; costs are subtracted; the tool
    says "no trade" when there isn't one. Several ideas were tested and dropped because the
    evidence didn't support them.
  - Regime awareness: signals are judged against comparable Fed-hiking environments, not
    against whatever history happens to be in the window.
  - Correct market mechanics: quarterly contract rolls, generic vs fixed contracts, and
    contract-level exposure are handled properly — common sources of false signals.
  - Automation end to end: live feed, history database, reports and checks run without
    manual data work.
  - Designed for focus: compact, readable, no unnecessary scrolling — built for long sessions.


----------------------------------------------------------------------
8. IMPACT ON MY TRADING
----------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Faster preparation: the morning routine went from juggling workbooks to one screen.
  - Better decisions: entries are based on ranked, tested evidence instead of feel.
  - Better risk control: exposure is visible at the 3M-spread level; stops and targets are
    planned from historical behaviour.
  - Better communication: every trade can be explained to a mentor with data in a few lines.
  - Learning loop: track records and backtests show which setups actually work, so I keep
    the ones with an edge and drop the rest.
